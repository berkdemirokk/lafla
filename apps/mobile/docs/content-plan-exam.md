# Lafla Exam Prep Track — Content Plan

## Introduction

This document specifies the 60 speaking scenarios that power Lafla's Exam Prep track for IELTS Speaking and TOEFL Speaking. Users purchase a 60-day Exam Pass for $99 with a money-back guarantee tied to test outcomes, which means every prompt, every band descriptor, and every evaluation rubric in this catalog is part of a contractual promise. A learner reaching for IELTS Band 7.0 or TOEFL Speaking 24+ should encounter prompts that feel indistinguishable in structure, register, and cognitive load from what they will face in a real Cambridge or ETS testing room. The scenarios below mirror the rhythm of authentic exam tasks (timing, cue-card phrasing, follow-up depth) while using original wording to avoid copyright issues and to keep content fresh across cohorts.

## Content Authenticity Notes

Every scenario above is written original-text but follows the structural DNA of authentic Cambridge IELTS and ETS TOEFL prompts. For IELTS Part 1 we mirror the 2–3 question topical micro-clusters (Hometown, Work/Study, Hobbies) that examiners actually use, keeping the register conversational and personal. Part 2 cue cards follow the exact "Describe a... You should say: ...; ...; ...; and explain why..." formula that has been stable in the Cambridge test for over a decade, so candidates build muscle memory for the visual layout they will see on the real cue card. Part 3 questions deliberately target the abstract/societal turn that examiners make, with hedging-friendly phrasing that rewards Band 7+ candidates who can speculate and qualify.

For TOEFL, Task 1 prompts use the canonical "Describe / Which do you prefer / Do you agree" stems and require 4–5 sentence answers built from a clear opinion plus 2 reasons plus 1 example. Task 2 scenarios are anchored to plausible campus-life scenarios (housing, dining, library, attendance, fees, recycling) because ETS draws from this narrow band of contexts in every test administration. Task 3 lectures cover the four-discipline rotation (biology, psychology, business/economics, environmental/social science) that ETS uses in published practice tests.

We deliberately avoid prompts that touch on partisan politics, religion, sensitive geopolitical conflicts, or personal trauma — both because real examiners avoid those zones and because our money-back guarantee means we cannot risk a learner freezing in a culturally sensitive scenario. Where a topic could veer (e.g., "traditions" in Part 3), we phrase it in neutral, universal terms that elicit balanced argumentation rather than identity-laden response.

All prompts will be reviewed quarterly against the latest publicly released Cambridge and ETS sample tests to ensure structural drift is caught early. Audio scripts for TOEFL Task 2 and Task 3 will be produced in the audio-content pipeline using two distinct voice actors (one campus, one academic) to match real test conditions.
