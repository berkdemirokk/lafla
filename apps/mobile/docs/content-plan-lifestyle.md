# Lafla — Daily Life Content Plan

## Purpose & Persona

This document is the content brief for Lafla's **Daily Life** track: 40 spoken-English scenarios that prepare an 18–35 year-old traveler, junior professional, or international student to navigate real conversations in English-speaking contexts. Source languages span TR/EN/IT/ES/DE/SR; the practice language is **English only** — all dialog, prompts, and target responses are authored in natural, current English (US/UK neutral where possible, with regional notes when relevant).

The track is built around situations the persona is statistically likely to encounter in their first 6–18 months living, working, or traveling in an English-speaking environment. Scenarios are short (4–12 turns), scoped to a single goal, and stack from concrete transactional language (A1–A2) into negotiation, nuance, and social register (B1–B2). A small number reach C1 where the social or professional stakes justify it.

## Design Notes

### (a) Progression — A1 entry, B2 ceiling

- **A1 entry points (3 scenarios):** `travel-airport-checkin`, `home-package-delivery`, and the simplest framing of `phone-voice-note-running-late`. These are the recommended "first contact" scenarios for an absolute beginner — single-goal, predictable script, very forgiving of errors.
- **A2 backbone (~14 scenarios):** the bulk of transactional life. Café orders, taxis, pharmacy, scheduling. A learner who completes A2 across all 8 categories can survive a 2-week trip abroad.
- **B1 core (~14 scenarios):** problem-solving and light negotiation. Things start going slightly wrong, and the user has to repair. This is the level where the app earns its keep.
- **B2 ceiling (~9 scenarios):** social nuance, disagreement, money disputes, professional pushback. We deliberately stop at B2 in v1 — C1+ (e.g., debate, sarcasm, code-switching) belongs in a future advanced track and is better served by IELTS Speaking content (see (c)).

### (b) Tag taxonomy — recurring families

Tags are intentionally reused across categories so the spaced-repetition engine and the data flywheel can cluster practice across contexts. Core recurring families:

- **`polite_requests`** — appears in Cafés, Travel, Shopping, Healthcare, Housing. The single most cross-cutting tag.
- **`complaints`** — Cafés, Shopping, Healthcare, Housing, Phone. Same emotional register, different domains.
- **`negotiation`** — Work, Travel, Shopping, Healthcare, Phone. Powers the B2 ceiling.
- **`small_talk`** — Cafés, Work, Travel, Dating, Housing. Glue language; cheap to learn, expensive to lack.
- **`exit_lines`** — Dating, Work watercooler, Social. Underrated; often missing from textbook content.
- **`scheduling` / `time` / `money` / `numbers`** — utility tags; appear almost everywhere.
- **`problem_solving`** — meta-tag for any scenario where the goal isn't a transaction but a fix.
- **`register` / `hedging`** — the "softness" axis. Pairs naturally with `disagreement`, `rejection`, `complaints`.

### (c) Dual-purpose candidates — Daily Life → IELTS / professional bridges

Several scenarios produce dialog and vocabulary that map cleanly onto exam-style or professional-track content. Flagged for reuse when we build those tracks:

- **IELTS Speaking Part 2 (the long turn / describe a…):** `shop-salon-haircut-spec`, `home-viewing-questions`, `social-group-hangout-intro`, `travel-rebook-cancelled-flight` all generate natural "describe a time when…" monologues.
- **IELTS Speaking Part 3 (abstract discussion):** `work-disagree-meeting`, `health-insurance-dispute`, `phone-cancel-subscription`, `social-say-no-second-date` ladder up into opinion/argument territory.
- **Business English bridge:** `work-disagree-meeting`, `work-ask-deadline-extension`, `work-standup-update`, `shop-handyman-quote` are direct reuse for a future Work track without rewriting.
- **TOEFL Integrated bridge:** less direct, but `health-describe-symptoms` and `home-viewing-questions` build the listen-then-summarize muscle.

### Open questions / follow-ups

- **Dating & Social framing:** the category title risks narrowing the persona. Recommend renaming the user-facing label to **"Meeting People"** while keeping `social-` as the internal id prefix. The content is already framed as everyday socializing, but the label matters for adoption.
- **Healthcare regional variance:** US vs. UK vs. EU healthcare vocabulary (GP vs. PCP, A&E vs. ER, insurance vs. NHS) diverges sharply. Recommend a v1.1 pass where we author regional variants for the 5 healthcare scenarios, or pick one variant and label it explicitly.
- **Voice-note category:** `phone-voice-note-running-late` is the only voice-note scenario. If voice-notes are a growing real-world genre (which I believe they are for this persona), we may want a small standalone subcategory in v2.
- **C1 ceiling:** we currently top out at B2. A small number of users will plateau here and want stretch content. Worth a learner-research check at 60 days post-launch.
